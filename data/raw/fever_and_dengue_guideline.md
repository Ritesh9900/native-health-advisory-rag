# Fever and Dengue Infection: Clinical Advisory

Source: World Health Organization (WHO) & Centers for Disease Control (CDC)
Authority: High (Clinical Advisory)
Audience: Patients & Caregivers
Review Date: 2026-02-01

## Understanding Fever
A normal body temperature fluctuates between 36.5°C and 37.5°C (97.7°F to 99.5°F). A body temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher indicates a fever. Fever is an immune response to fighting infections, most commonly viral or bacterial.

## Dengue Fever: Overview & Early Symptoms
Dengue is a viral infection transmitted to humans through the bites of infected Aedes mosquitoes.
Common symptoms include:
- High fever (often sudden onset up to 40°C / 104°F)
- Severe headache and severe pain behind the eyes (retro-orbital pain)
- Intense muscle and joint pains (often called "breakbone fever")
- Nausea, vomiting, and skin rash

## Critical Medication Warning for Dengue
- **SAFE**: Paracetamol (acetaminophen) can be used to alleviate pain and lower fever under medical supervision.
- **DANGER - DO NOT TAKE**: Avoid Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) such as Ibuprofen, Aspirin, Diclofenac, or Naproxen. NSAIDs thin the blood and can dramatically increase the risk of severe internal bleeding and hemorrhage in dengue patients.

## Dengue Warning Signs: Emergency Protocol
Severe dengue is a life-threatening medical emergency that typically develops 3 to 7 days after the first symptoms, often as the fever begins to drop.
Go to the nearest emergency department immediately if you or someone else has:
- Severe abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting (at least 3 times in 24 hours)
- Bleeding from the gums or nose, or blood in vomit or stool
- Extreme fatigue, restlessness, or altered mental state
- Rapid breathing, cold or clammy extremities (signs of dengue shock)
