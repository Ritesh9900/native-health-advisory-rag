# Type 2 Diabetes: Patient Health Advisory & Management

Source: MedlinePlus & World Health Organization (WHO)
Authority: High (Public Health Guideline)
Audience: Patients & Caregivers
Review Date: 2026-01-15

## Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic condition that affects the way your body metabolizes glucose (sugar). With type 2 diabetes, the body either resists the effects of insulin or doesn't produce enough insulin to maintain normal glucose levels.

## Common Symptoms
- Increased thirst (polydipsia) and frequent urination (polyuria), especially at night
- Increased hunger (polyphagia)
- Unintended weight loss
- Fatigue and weakness
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores or frequent infections
- Areas of darkened skin, usually in the armpits and neck (acanthosis nigricans)

## Lifestyle & Dietary Guidance
1. **Nutrition**: Focus on high-fiber foods such as non-starchy vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and lean proteins. Limit refined carbohydrates, sugary drinks, and processed snacks.
2. **Physical Activity**: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity (such as brisk walking) per week.
3. **Blood Glucose Monitoring**: Regularly check blood sugar as directed by your healthcare team to understand how food and activity affect your levels.
4. **Foot Care**: Inspect feet daily for cuts, blisters, red spots, and swelling. Never walk barefoot.

## When to Seek Urgent Medical Care (Red Flags)
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if you experience:
- Extreme confusion or lethargy
- Deep, rapid breathing with a fruity odor on the breath (signs of Diabetic Ketoacidosis / DKA)
- Persistent vomiting preventing fluid intake
- Blood glucose levels consistently above 300 mg/dL accompanied by ketone presence
- High fever and signs of severe infection or non-healing foot ulcers with spreading redness
