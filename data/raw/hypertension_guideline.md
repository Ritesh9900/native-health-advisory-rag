# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure): Management & Safety

Source: World Health Organization (WHO) & ICMR Guidelines
Authority: High (Public Health Guideline)
Audience: Patients & Public
Review Date: 2026-02-10

## Understanding Blood Pressure Readings
Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers:
- **Systolic (top number)**: Pressure when your heart beats.
- **Diastolic (bottom number)**: Pressure when your heart rests between beats.

Categories:
- **Normal**: Less than 120/80 mmHg
- **Elevated**: Systolic 120–129 and Diastolic less than 80
- **Hypertension Stage 1**: Systolic 130–139 or Diastolic 80–89 mmHg
- **Hypertension Stage 2**: Systolic 140 or higher, or Diastolic 90 or higher mmHg
- **Hypertensive Crisis**: Systolic higher than 180 and/or Diastolic higher than 120 mmHg (Requires immediate emergency intervention if accompanied by symptoms)

## Why It Is Called the "Silent Killer"
High blood pressure frequently causes no symptoms for years, even while damaging arteries, the heart, brain, and kidneys. Do not wait for symptoms before getting your blood pressure checked.

## Non-Pharmacological Management
- **Sodium Restriction**: Reduce salt intake to less than 5 grams (about one level teaspoon) per day.
- **DASH Diet**: Emphasize fruits, vegetables, whole grains, nuts, and low-fat dairy while minimizing saturated fats.
- **Weight Control & Physical Activity**: Engage in 30 minutes of moderate physical activity (brisk walking, swimming) at least 5 days a week.
- **Stress & Alcohol**: Limit alcohol consumption, avoid smoking, and practice stress-reduction techniques (deep breathing, yoga, meditation).

## Hypertensive Emergencies
Seek immediate emergency medical help if blood pressure is over 180/120 mmHg and you notice:
- Severe chest pain
- Severe throbbing headache with confusion or blurred vision
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Seizures or unresponsiveness
