# Headaches & Migraines: Types, Relief & Red Flags

Source: MedlinePlus & National Health Service (NHS)
Authority: High (Clinical Guideline)
Audience: Patients & Caregivers
Review Date: 2026-02-15

## Common Types of Headaches
- **Tension Headaches**: The most common type. Often described as a tight band around the forehead or back of the head. Usually triggered by stress, eye strain, dehydration, or poor posture.
- **Migraines**: Moderate to severe throbbing pain, usually on one side of the head. Often accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and extreme sensitivity to light and sound.
- **Cluster Headaches**: Severe, piercing pain around or behind one eye, recurring in cycles.

## General Self-Care & Non-Pharmacological Relief
1. **Hydration & Rest**: Drink plenty of water and rest in a quiet, dark, well-ventilated room.
2. **Cold or Warm Compress**: Apply a cool cloth or ice pack wrapped in a towel to the forehead or neck. For tension headaches, a warm compress or warm shower can relax neck muscles.
3. **Trigger Management**: Avoid skipped meals, reduce caffeine fluctuations, and manage screen time and sleep consistency.

## Safe Medication Practices
- Mild, occasional headaches may be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers such as paracetamol or acetaminophen under guidance.
- Frequent use of painkillers (more than 2 to 3 days per week) can paradoxically cause "medication-overuse headaches" (rebound headaches). Always consult a healthcare provider for recurring headaches.

## Red Flag Symptoms: When to Seek Immediate Emergency Care
Go to the nearest emergency room immediately if a headache:
- Comes on suddenly and reaches severe intensity within seconds (a "thunderclap headache")
- Is accompanied by a high fever, stiff neck, confusion, or rash
- Is accompanied by weakness, numbness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking (signs of a stroke)
- Develops after a severe head injury or trauma
- Is new or worsening in a person over 50 or someone with an active cancer diagnosis
